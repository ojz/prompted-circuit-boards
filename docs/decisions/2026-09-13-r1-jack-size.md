# R1: which jacks face the outside world?

## Decision Needed

R1, the IO + Mixer board, is the one board whose jacks connect to things
that are not Eurorack: the audio interface on the laptop, headphones, a
mixer, a synth with line outputs. Its Eurorack-side jacks are 3.5 mm
Thonkiconns like every other board; the question is the world side. The
choice fixes the board's width (a 6.35 mm jack needs about twice the panel
area of a 3.5 mm one), its footprint library, its panel holes and whether a
balanced connection is possible at all. The R1 SPEC cannot be written
without it ([../ROADMAP.md](../ROADMAP.md), P2 and "Decisions outstanding").

## Options And Recommendation

| Option | What it gives | What it costs |
|---|---|---|
| **A. 6.35 mm TS (mono, unbalanced) jacks** | Plugs straight into any audio interface line input or output, mixer, amp or pedal with an ordinary guitar cable. The standard for line-level gear. | About 19 mm of panel pitch per jack against 13.7 mm for a Thonkiconn, so four world-side jacks add roughly 20 mm of panel height or 4 HP of width; PCB-mount 6.35 mm jacks are through-hole and hand-soldered like the Thonkiconns. |
| **B. 6.35 mm TRS balanced** | Rejects hum on long cables to a balanced interface input; the professional standard. | Doubles the output circuitry (a balanced driver per output), adds a part to model and a deck, and gains nothing on a cable under two metres to an interface next to the case. |
| **C. 3.5 mm on both sides** | Smallest, cheapest, one footprint for the whole board. | Every connection to the outside world needs an adapter or a 3.5-to-6.35 cable; 3.5 mm plugs are the least robust connector on the bench. |
| **D. RCA** | Consumer hi-fi and some mixers. | Rare on interfaces; nothing else in the plan uses it. |

The recommendation is **A**: 6.35 mm TS, unbalanced, for the inputs and the
outputs. The interface planned for Stage B of the lab ([../HOMELAB.md](../HOMELAB.md))
and almost every line-level device the board will meet accept it directly,
the cable run is a metre, and the board's job is to be the world interface
of a small case, not a studio patch bay. Balanced (B) is a later board if a
long cable ever becomes a problem.

Still unknown, and not for the user to supply: the exact jack part and its
footprint (the agent chooses one with an official KiCad footprint and a
verified LCSC or Thonk source), and the number of world-side jacks, which
follows from the mix semantics decision beside this one.

Acceptance check for the chosen option: the R1 SPEC lists the part, its
panel pitch and hole size, the panel fits the agreed width, and the panel
project passes `toolkit/check.sh io-mixer panel`.
